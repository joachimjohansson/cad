It started with a call from an engineer at a small pump company. Their cast iron valve housing worked, but when they tested the new pump the pressure dropped more than they had calculated. Nobody wanted to redesign the whole unit, so they asked me to look at the housing alone.

The problem sat in the inlet. The casting went from a straight channel straight into a sharp corner, and the liquid had to hit the wall before it could continue. I first redrew the housing as it looked on paper, keeping the same outer dimensions and flanges so that the mounting would not change.

The hard part was changing the inner shape without making the walls thicker. I made a sketch where the flow took a soft curve instead of a knee, and I wrote a note in the margin: the flow should not be slowed here. I wanted whoever read the drawing to understand why that curve mattered. Then I tested three radii in a simple flow calculation and chose the middle one, since it kept almost the same weight as the original.

When the section drawing was finished, the foundry could see where the wall could be thinned and where it had to stay. The first prototype casting took a few weeks, and the test run showed that the pressure held. The housing is a few hundred grams lighter than before. What I still like most is how clearly the curve shows in the section, because that is where the whole idea lives.
