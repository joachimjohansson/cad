A maintenance company called one winter to say that their mounting brackets were coming back with cracks at the bend. They had been welding three pieces together for each bracket, and the welds became brittle when the units stood outside in the cold. The request was for one bracket that could be made from a single sheet and carry the same load.

I started by drawing it in isometric view, so that anyone mounting it could see how it sat against the wall. The base got four screw holes, and the upright got two long slots. The slots were the part I was least sure about, since they make the load harder to calculate. But the installers needed to adjust the height by a few millimetres when a wall was not straight.

The decision that mattered most was the bend radius. A sharp corner looked clean on the drawing, but I chose R30 so the sheet would not split when it was bent. The side view got every dimension, so that the workshop could check the part without having to call me.

The first batch came back without a single complaint. I like that about this kind of job. The best parts are the ones nobody notices, because they simply do what they are supposed to do.
